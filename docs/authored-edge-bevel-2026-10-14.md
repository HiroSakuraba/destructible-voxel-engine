# Authored edge bevel, open boundaries and concave folds

This batch combines persistent-element provenance for edge bevel/chamfer/rounded-fillet tools with bounded local open-surface reconstruction. Native Polygon and Subdivision actions use the new `bevel_authored_editor_edges` API; the earlier Manifold convex-bevel compatibility API remains unchanged. The new path does not require the Manifold backend.

## Supported geometry

- Closed, outward-wound convex solids use explicit half-space clipping and generated profile patches. Multiple selected uninterrupted physical edges can meet at convex corners; each successive cut reconstructs its cap boundary. A physical crease divided by loop cuts can instead be beveled as one complete straight chain per operation. Its two face planes must agree along the chain. The profile is cut once and split into axial slabs at the original segment junctions, with explicit triangle/quad records and per-segment rail descendants. Additional rail points are inserted into neighboring patches with their own source attributes, preserving oblique source boundaries without T-junctions. Geometry intersections are keyed by the source geometric edge and cutting step, not recovered by matching old positions.
- Open folded surfaces support one straight, unbranched chain of two-face crease edges, including convex and concave folds. The two face sides must be consistent planar quad domains, with perpendicular end rails. The chain ends must be open: additional incident faces, branches and bent-chain miters are refused.
- Open boundary chains roll a planar border out of its original plane. The inner rail is set back into the face; the new outer border is lowered by the same setback along the negative face normal. Depth zero connects them with a flat chamfer; Depth one uses a quarter-circle profile. This changes the border geometry rather than adding a flat triangulation strip. It does not add wall thickness or close the surface.

The local open paths preserve non-affine UV0/UV1 and color fields, including separate attributes on the two render triangles of a saved quad. Those faces retain their actual source triangle domains during clipping. A render diagonal crossing the new rail adds a shared axial column to the opposite rail and all profile rows, avoiding T-junctions. Each profile interval uses the attribute copy from its own source triangle and face side, so diagonal discontinuities and seams between adjacent chain quads survive. Affine, internally unsplit faces keep the compact quad path when both sides allow it. Unchanged corners retain their original attribute values; new rails interpolate their own source-side fields. Both UV channels remain independent. Closed clipping also retains source triangle domains and interpolates clipping intersections on their actual edges.

Closed concave solids and capped concave endpoint joins are not implemented. Neither are arbitrary bent-chain joins, advanced miters, editable construction history or a direct CAD-radius parameter. Incomplete collinear crease selections are refused: select every segment of the physical crease. Combining a split chain with other selected creases is also refused until their interacting corner joins have dedicated reconstruction. Existing multi-edge bevels on uninterrupted creases remain supported. This does not consolidate or dissolve the source subdivisions.

## Width and profile

Existing Width, fractional/absolute mode, local/world space, Segments (1–16), Depth (0–1), Auto Fit and Smoothing Angle controls remain. Closed-convex absolute width retains the earlier bisector-offset convention; the two-face open path uses the corresponding face setback. The one-face boundary path uses width directly as its setback and border drop. Fractional width and Auto Fit use a conservative shortest-control-edge bound, not a maximum-fit solver.

Nonuniform world scale is applied during reconstruction and removed when writing local positions. Generated profile shading is computed on the final local geometry. Smoothing affects new profile attributes; surviving source normals and tangents remain unchanged.

## Identity and metadata contract

Every emitted attribute has an explicit geometric group. Source attributes, triangles and corners carry construction-time parents. Unchanged authored faces retain their memberships; changed faces emit deliberate triangle/quad records and explicit child-face mappings. Triangulated clipping patches preserve collinear boundary subdivisions without producing zero-area faces. Four-corner profile patches use quad records; larger clipped patches and corner joins use explicit supported triangles rather than inventing authored n-gons. Preservation applies to surviving cage geometry and attributes, not an unchanged subdivision limit surface: changed control-face records can change the evaluated smooth shape.

Untouched elements retain IDs even when output arrays are compacted or reordered. New geometry/attributes and replacement profile faces receive fresh IDs. Each selected segment maps to its own replacement boundary rails; original edges shortened by a cut map through their known source-edge intersections. New intermediate profile edges and corner joins start smooth. Crease strengths and both saved seam channels follow supported edge descendants. Conflicting material assignments across the two sides of a selected segment, or conflicting crease strengths on a shared descendant, refuse the operation. Materials and crease strengths may differ between successive chain segments and remain confined to their own profile slabs and rails.

Pins on surviving source corners follow their explicit emitted copies. Pins whose corners are retired refuse remapping; they are not guessed onto nearby profile corners. New profile corners start unpinned. New UV/color values derive from source-side fields, not a new unwrap, packing or texel-density solution. Copied rails can produce collapsed UV strips that need a subsequent UV projection/unwrap. Identical material definitions/bindings in separate palette slots are compatible.

Native previews compute migration and provisional IDs in a local snapshot. They do not install geometry, records or allocation reservations on the source object. Cancel therefore restores the source by discarding the preview, including for ID-less meshes. Changing source geometry, topology or subdivision settings requires closing/reopening the preview so stale array selections cannot be rebound. Apply revalidates saved UV state and commits geometry, prepared subdivision settings, identity records and UV flags as one compound transaction. Undo restores the exact source record/selection and keeps the committed allocation high-water mark; Redo restores exact output IDs. Prefab topology still requires editing the source or detaching the instance. Coincident independent partitions remain refused by native position-based algorithms.

## Validation and budgets

Focused core checks cover closed convex chamfers/rounded profiles, multi-edge convex corners, open convex/concave folds, straight multi-edge chains, one-face boundary rolls, ordinary polygon meshes and authored cages. They check valid connectivity, explicit identity/crease/seam mappings, dual-channel pin handling, width-independent provisional IDs, nonuniform world width, subdivision evaluation, and bevel after loop cut. A cube's single-edge absolute chamfer retains the legacy 0.99 volume result at width 0.1, including after one, two or three loop cuts divide that crease. Chain checks cover disjoint per-segment rails/crease strengths and seam channels, persistent selection resolution, surviving pins, profile slab boundaries, explicit T-junction detection, width-stable provisional IDs, ordinary polygons, oblique/sheared cages, nonuniform world scale and segment-specific materials. Near-collinear rail points stay in larger triangulation ears; clipping reuses endpoints already on a profile plane to avoid duplicate near-coincident intersections.

Refusal cases include invalid width/scale, partial collinear creases, closed concave joins, skew rails, bent chains and material conflicts. Non-affine and internally split open domains are now covered by independent source-triangle interpolation checks, explicit T-junction detection, profile/source attribute-copy checks, active subdivision evaluation, width-stable provisional IDs and world-scale comparisons. This preserves the source cage fields, not an unchanged subdivided limit surface: retaining triangle domains can introduce additional authored triangle/quad faces. Real compound-command tests exercise identified and ID-less Apply/Undo/Redo with prepared IDs and both seam channels, including split-domain concave chains, boundary rolls and two- and four-segment closed crease chains; actual beveled meshes, identity records and seam states also save/reopen correctly, including split chains. Native integration coverage is registered for preview/Cancel, chamfer/rounded actions, Enter Apply, selection restoration and exact Undo/Redo; full controller runtime and visual acceptance were not executed locally. Native sources and tests were syntax-checked.

Limits are 100,000 attribute entries, 50,000 triangles and 256 selected profile segments. Output budgets are enforced during emission. Each convex cut contour is limited to 512 vertices; convexity verification is bounded to two million plane/vertex incidences. The tool checks local geometry, manifold incidence and explicit geometric partitions. It does not certify global surface self-intersection freedom.

Manual acceptance: insert a loop in a cube cage, select every segment of a divided physical crease and mark surviving corner pins in both UV channels, then preview chamfer and rounded profiles. Repeat on an uninterrupted crease and compare the cut volume. Give neighboring segments different crease strengths and saved seam channels; verify their own rails retain them. Change width, segments and smoothing; inspect the checker, rail creases, source shading and selection. Apply/Undo/Redo and save/reopen. Repeat on a straight open concave fold and a quad border, checking the documented border-drop direction. Repeat with a non-affine checker and split UV/color values across the saved quad diagonal; inspect source/profile joins and seams between consecutive quads. Try an incomplete chain and several split chains meeting at a corner, then a bent open chain, pinned retired corner and conflicting material; verify the source remains intact and the refusal reason is visible.
